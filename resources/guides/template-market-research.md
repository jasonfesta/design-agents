# Popular website templates and category research

> Current model (October 13): [type → device → size → category → subcategory → style](../structure.md). App product categories now sit above the preserved interface families; website/game interfaces have their own subcategory menus. Earlier decisions and examples below are dated research.

October 12, 2026 · Public-source research for Jason · Website scope · Proposed naming guidance

The strongest starting set for further design research is portfolios, agencies, software marketing, commerce, services and booking, and education. These recur in the selected popular collections or provide useful contrasts within them. This is a research priority based on a small sample, not a ranking of market demand. The existing [18 website categories](../websites/categories.md) remain approved; the sample supplies finer use cases and visual descriptions within them.

Jason narrowed the task to the providers' leading templates, top categories, and actual sub-level categories. This record therefore concentrates on Shopify, Framer, Wix, and Squarespace's popular surfaces. Catalog size is retained only as context. No style has been selected as Jason's preference; [personal coverage](../../agents/memory/COVERAGE.md) remains open.

Navigation prominence, category inventory size, popularity, sales, and customer satisfaction are different signals. The category paths below are actual provider labels or explicitly identified interpretations; their position does not prove market share.

## Shopify

Shopify explicitly highlights Clothing, Beauty, Jewelry and accessories, and Home as its top industries. Its useful second dimension is operational: catalog size and shopping features. The observed catalog-size choices are One Product, Few (2–10), Some (11–100+), and Lots (500+). Preserve those source labels as written rather than inventing continuous ranges. [Theme filters](https://themes.shopify.com/themes)

The first five entries in the observed trending collection were **Heritage, Refresh, Dwell, Atelier, and Impulse**. Symmetry also appeared later. These provide a compact contrast set:

| Template and author | Design/use signal | Useful research direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Heritage — Shopify](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/heritage/presets/heritage) | Editorial presentation, asymmetrical galleries, emphasis on material and craftsmanship | Product storytelling; expressive composition |
| [Refresh — Shopify](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/refresh/presets/refresh) | Strong headings, restrained palette, ingredients and product-quality information | Clear product communication; assertive typography |
| [Dwell — Shopify](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/dwell/presets/dwell) | Warm neutrals, serif typography, large catalogs and vertical filters | Warm presentation combined with substantial product discovery |
| [Atelier — Shopify](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/atelier/presets/atelier) | Spacious galleries and large editorial media | Boutique commerce; restrained luxury intent |
| [Impulse — Archetype Themes](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/impulse/presets/impulse) | Fashion/apparel, shoppable imagery, promotional sections | Merchandising and campaign-driven commerce |
| [Symmetry — Clean Canvas](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/symmetry/presets/symmetry) | Fashion positioning, product filters; seller claims use by over 70,000 stores | A longer-established commerce reference; adoption claim is seller-reported |

The meaningful next level is often **theme → preset**, rather than a deeper industry tree. Impulse includes Impulse, Dune, Terrain, and Apothecary; Symmetry includes Symmetry, Salt Yard, Beatnik, Duke, and Amara. These appeared separately in the trending collection. Retain their parent theme to avoid treating each preset as an unrelated design system.

For our kit, these belong primarily in `ecommerce`. Industry, catalog scale, navigation, promotions, product media, and preset identity remain fields. A beauty storefront can require a completely different structure from a bookable beauty service.

In our records these become composition, type, layout, palette, mode, rendering, and motion fields respectively. “Animated” needs a specific behavior before it becomes meaningful guidance.

Wix supplies a useful naming pattern: **specific use + appearance modifier**. The first part tells us the content and business task; the second starts a visual conversation. For our kit, service sites map to `business-services`, booking-led experiences to `booking-reservations`, and work showcases to `portfolios`. Keep Wix's “Industrial” parent as source metadata rather than adding it to our approved website-use menu.

## Proposed research priorities and naming

Keep six practical research groups within the approved website menu:

| Research priority | Specific uses to compare | Existing category home |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creative portfolios | Product designer; photographer; stylist; creative director | `portfolios` |
| Agencies and studios | Design studio; architecture studio; marketing agency | `agency-studio` |
| Software marketing | AI agent product; SaaS; app launch; waitlist | `saas-marketing`, `app-launches`, or `landing-pages`, according to deliverable |
| Commerce | Fashion; beauty; jewelry; home; boutique versus broad catalog | `ecommerce` |
| Services and booking | Consultant; legal practice; home service; salon; wellness coach | `business-services` or `booking-reservations` |
| Education and membership | Course; children's learning; coaching program; recurring access | `courses-education` or `community-membership` |

Jason subsequently singled out portfolios: “Should grab categories like portfolios, things like that. I think that's interesting.” Portfolios therefore lead the next research pass. The [category menu's template research focus](../websites/categories.md#template-research-focus) captures the narrower uses and starting references. The remaining sampling order is proposed, not a new category approval. Narrower uses remain metadata unless Jason later approves a structural change.

Start visual vocabulary from repeated evidence: **Editorial, Minimalism, and International Typographic Style** already have identities in the [shared vocabulary](../styles/README.md). Use monochrome, warm neutrals, dark, large type, asymmetrical grid, photography, and illustration as explicit modifiers. Treat premium, sophisticated, polished, friendly, and playful as intent until references show how they are expressed. A design-software-inspired portfolio is a useful reference description; one product listing does not establish a universal style category.

An example research record could read: **Portfolios → product designer → Editorial**, with source Nudge, design-software references, project metadata, and case-study structure in fields. This is an interpretation to test. Nudge's author-specific wording remains attached to its source, and its appearance is not automatically classified as Swiss Style.

For each selected reference retain provider, author, source URL, observed date, popular surface/time window, source category path, theme/preset identity, site purpose, industry, appearance terms, page structure, and inspection level. Preserve motion claims separately until the live behavior is viewed. These are fields inside the existing `type/viewport/category/style` structure, not new folder layers.

## Scope and next application

The strongest evidence here is collection membership, selected order/time window, actual category labels, template descriptions, and three Squarespace desktop previews plus Framer's leading gallery thumbnails. This is not a full visual, mobile, accessibility, motion, or conversion audit. Popularity does not establish design quality or Jason's taste.

The next useful design step is a small comparison within one of the six research groups: select two or three concrete references, identify the visible differences, and record Jason's keep/change/avoid feedback.
